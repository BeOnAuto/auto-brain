# 0014 — A warm worker pool: a run costs its own work, in a worker kept between jobs and let go of after any bad one

**Status:** proposed (2026-10-06), rewritten the same day; built 2026-10-07, with the amendments at the end

## Context

The engine's `dsl` entry gives every capability that evaluates programs one pool of worker threads (`packages/workflow-engine/src/program-pool/program-pool.ts`): `run` evaluates one program and `fold` one page of folds, each under the pool's permits (`pool-slots.ts`), a heap of 256 MiB and a stack of 64 MiB, and a deadline the evaluator keeps inside its work charge and the pool keeps by terminating the thread. Today the pool starts a thread for every job and terminates it after the answer (`program-pool.ts:171,178`); the worker module reads `workerData` and posts one answer at import (`program-worker.ts:5-8`, `fold-worker.ts:10-19`), so a thread cannot take a second job; and the module is chosen for each request (`program-pool.ts:188,196`): the computation primitive names its own module so the output is checked against its schema where the program ran (`primitives/computation/src/run/output-worker.ts`), the recollection primitive one module for folds and one for answers (`primitives/recollection/src/run/fold-worker.ts`, `answer-worker.ts`), the host's tests a fourth (`packages/workflow-host/src/views-testing/test-fold-worker.ts`) and a fifth written as a data URL (`view-documents.ts:25-38`); the three capability modules differ only by the check they pass. Decision 0005's amendment chose one worker for each run so that no state passes from one run to the next and a terminated worker never has to be replaced.

The cost is measured. A run of a program that answers at once took 27.5 ms at the median with its worker started and ended, most of it the worker stripping the types of its modules as it loads them, and roughly 120 to 160 ms when the function has an output schema, because that worker also loads `effect` and the schema compiler, 163.9 ms at the median of 30 under load (`primitives/computation/README.md:50,57`). A fold worker took 40 to 280 ms to start when measured alone, and a rebuild of the example view over 100,000 matching events read 111 to 113 pages of up to 1,000 records and took 103.5 s on SQLite and 111.1 s on PostgreSQL, about 0.95 s a page (`packages/workflow-host/README.md:145`, `docs/reference/recall-format.md:231`). The example's own work is 4.0 ms on the largest input a run takes, so a computation run with a schema spends thirty to forty times its work on starting a thread. The computation README and the reference page both name a pool that keeps workers warm as what comes next, and the prediction record (0012, section 5) plans a session worker on it: one artifact's session kept for runs of that artifact, idle a minute, recycled after 1,000 runs and at once after any failed, disqualified or terminated run, never more than the permits.

What the evaluator keeps between evaluations, verified against the code: `activeTracker`, a module-level variable the patch sets for each evaluation and restores in a `finally` (`patches/@gabrielbryk__jq-ts@1.7.0.patch:87,1058-1068`); three caches that are `WeakMap`s keyed by the values they describe, `knownSizes` and `knownDepths` in the patch (lines 44 and 67), `measures` in `src/dsl/json.ts:97` and `converted` in `src/programs/program-running.ts:45`, whose entries go with the values; the regex cache, a field of the tracker made for each evaluation (patch lines 150 and 1049); and the work meter, `usage`, made for each call of `program.run` (`program-compiling.ts:59-61`). A program is compiled for every job (`program-answer.ts:105`) and a fold once a page (`fold-page.ts:100`); a JSON Schema is compiled for every job (`primitives/computation/src/run/output-check.ts:12`) and once a page (`value-checks.ts:12`). The library's run reads the parse tree and never writes to it (no assignment to a node's field in the installed `dist/index.mjs`), and a program that writes through `__proto__` by index, by `setpath` or in an object literal leaves `Object.prototype` untouched (a probe over the installed library; the dotted forms do not parse). The portability test allows `node:worker_threads` in `src/program-pool` alone and keeps the main and testing entries from reaching the pool by following relative imports (`src/engine/portability.test.ts:20,140,155-166,187-192`); outside the engine, the five worker modules above and two measure scripts (`primitives/computation/measure/workers.ts:1`, `heap-worker.ts:2`) import it themselves, and the engine's and the host's tests keep inline workers that read `workerData` at import or post bare answers (`program-pool.test.ts:191-197,212-217`, `fold-pool.test.ts:32-34,111-113,154-156`, `lost-pages.test.ts:24-25,62,114`). The output check is written twice, the same in `output-check.ts:11-19` and `value-checks.ts:11-23`; its issues are put into words four times (`value-checks.ts:28`, `view-answer.ts:30-33`, `run-outcome.ts:97-99`, `answer-endings.ts:82-84`); the request a worker reads is decoded by hand twice (`program-answer.ts:28-62`, `fold-answer.ts:23-96`) where the answers already cross as `effect` schemas (`program-messages.ts`, `fold-messages.ts`); and the scripted pool of the tests exists twice (`primitives/computation/src/testing/scripted-pool.ts`, `primitives/recollection/src/testing/scripted-pool.ts`).

## Decision

### 1. A worker takes jobs as messages, and the pool keeps it between them

The pool keeps warm workers keyed by their module URL. A job takes a permit as today, then the most recently used idle worker of its module or, when there is none, starts one; it posts an envelope, `{ job, kind, request, progress }`, the job's id from a counter of the pool's, `program` or `fold`, the request as today's `workerData`, `context` among its fields, and for a fold its progress buffer, a `SharedArrayBuffer` that travels beside the request; the worker answers `{ job, answer, keep }`. The pool matches an answer to the job by its id: an answer naming a job not in flight on that worker, a message that is not an envelope, an error or an exit on a worker with no job in flight forgets that worker and ends nothing, and an answer that arrives after the pool terminated the worker is dropped. The worker module no longer reads `workerData`: `serveJobs({ program, fold })`, implemented in `src/program-pool/job-loop.ts` and exported from a Node-hosted path of its own, `@beonauto/workflow-engine/job-loop`, listens on the parent port, decodes each request with the schema the pool encodes it with, hands the whole request to the handler of its kind, and keeps in its closure a bounded cache of compiled programs keyed by the source and the dialect, 256 KiB of text as the workflow caller's (`src/dsl/expressions.ts:41-43`, through `boundedCacheOf`), and one of compiled checks keyed by the schema's JSON text, the same size, so a check is compiled once for each schema. The `worker` entry keeps the answerers, `answerOf`, `foldAnswerOf` and `progressOf`, and they stay portable for the hosted adapter. The engine's own two modules call `serveJobs` with the plain answerers, and one checked module serves both capabilities (section 4), so computation's output worker and recollection's fold and answer workers go; the host's two test workers and the inline workers of the engine's and the host's tests move to the envelope, the blocking ones excepted, since a worker that never answers needs no protocol. One warm set for each module URL, not one set with the module chosen for each job, because a thread's realm is the module it loaded. All the sets share the permits.

### 2. Isolation between jobs in one worker, and why no fresh realm is needed

A worker may keep between jobs its loaded modules, its compiled programs and its compiled checks, because compilation is a pure function of the text and the dialect and the tree is read, never written; nothing else. It keeps no value of a job, no tracker, no meter and no regex cache, and none of these outlives a job today: the tracker, its regex cache and the meter are made for each evaluation and the active tracker is restored on every exit; the caches of sizes, depths and conversions are weak maps keyed by values that are parsed fresh for each job (`program-answer.ts:109-110`, `fold-answer.ts:80`) and unreachable after it. A program reaches no host object: the dialect refuses `env`, `$ENV`, `input`, `now` and the rest, the worker's environment is empty (`program-pool.ts:127`), jq has no way to call into the host, and writing through `__proto__` changes nothing a later job sees. So a job needs no fresh realm, and the same program on the same input spends the same work in a warm worker as in a cold one. The heap limit holds for each job because it bounds the old generation, which garbage collection frees between jobs, and a worker that exceeds it ends with the `memory` verdict and is gone (`program-pool.ts:65,77-80`); the output and the view are measured for each job (`program-answer.ts:98`, `fold-page.ts:110`), and nothing accumulates. Each of these claims is a test of the build.

### 3. The lifecycle of a worker

The envelope says whether the worker may be kept, so the rule is the worker's kind's and 0012's session worker adds its own without a second rule in the pool. A program job keeps the worker when the evaluator returned with any verdict, answered, raised, exhausted by work, depth, value depth or the stack (a `RangeError` the evaluator catches, `program-compiling.ts:62-64`), unanswered, unfit, oversized, mismatched or refused, and not when it ended `exhausted` by the deadline, since the thread was busy past the job's deadline. A fold job keeps the worker when every view ended within its fold's deadline, and not when a view is `overtime` (`fold-page.ts:117-120`) or the page was `unreadable`. The pool lets a worker go, terminating it before its job's permit is released:

- after any job it stopped, by the deadline, the memory, a cancellation or the closing, after any job that crashed, and after any answer whose envelope says not to keep the worker;
- after its 1,000th job, and after a minute idle, on a timer that is unreferenced so it holds no process open, and cleared when the worker is taken.

Nothing is started before it is needed: the first job of a module pays the worker's start inside its own deadline, as decision 0005 counts it. Jobs never queue on a worker; they queue for a permit, first come first served, as today (`pool-slots.ts:59-66`), and a worker is let go of while its last job still holds the permit, so no job is ever assigned to a worker being recycled and the threads alive never exceed the permits. When a job's module has no idle worker and the threads alive equal the permits, the oldest idle worker of any module is terminated first; one exists, since the job holds a permit. A job's wait for a permit stays inside its deadline (`program-pool.ts:187-190`), and a page's wait and its deadline from the moment it is taken stay as they are (`fold-job.ts:36-47`). `close()` terminates every worker, idle and busy, and a busy job ends `closing`.

### 4. The pool's visible contract, and the one checked worker

`ProgramPool`, its `workers`, `heapMegabytes`, `run`, `fold` and `close`, `PoolSettings`, `ProgramRequest` with its `worker` and `context`, `FoldRequest` and every ending keep their shape and meaning, so the capabilities and the host's pool fakes change nothing but their worker modules, and see the same outcomes with smaller `milliseconds`. `PoolSettings` gains two settings for tests, never for the operator, `idleMs` and `jobsPerWorker`, 60,000 and 1,000 unless a test gives less; `COMPUTATION_WORKERS` stays the one operator setting, and now bounds the threads alive as well as the jobs at once. The request schemas join the answer schemas, and the hand-written readers go; `ProgramPool`'s types, the messages and `program-answer.ts` move out of `program-pool/` into `src/jobs/`, a portable folder, so one `scriptedPool` is exported from the engine's testing entry, which the portability test keeps from reaching the pool, and the two copies go. `@beonauto/specs/json-schema` gains `schemaCheckOf(schema, { what, nesting })`, answering a check that gives at most three issues, `issuesDetail(issues, what)`, the one wording of them that replaces the four copies, and `checkedWorker`, the URL of the one worker module that serves both capabilities: it calls `serveJobs` with `answerOf` under the output check of the request's `context` and `foldAnswerOf` under the view check of each view's schema. That adds a dependency of specs on the engine, which is acyclic: the engine depends on the ledger and the operations alone (`packages/workflow-engine/package.json:19-24`), the operations on no workspace package, and the ledger, which named specs among its development dependencies for one script alone (`packages/ledger/package.json:30-31`), names it nowhere once that script moves to specs (Build), since the build orders its tasks by every dependency of a package, scripts' among them. Computation's `checkedBy` and recollection's, and `recallFolding.worker`, name that URL, and `output-check.ts` and `value-checks.ts` go. A lint rule, `no-restricted-imports` for `node:worker_threads` with an override for `packages/workflow-engine/src/program-pool` and the computation measure scripts, keeps the port in the pool. The hosted runtime is untouched: `job-loop.ts` joins `dsl.ts` and `program-pool/` as Node-hosted in the portability test, the main, testing and `worker` entries reach none of them, and the hosted adapter offers neither computation nor recall until it bounds time and memory (0005, 0006). The session worker of 0012 builds on this by one extension: its key is the module URL and the artifact's hash, and its `keep` carries its own rule of failed, disqualified and terminated runs.

### 5. Measurement, before and after

Before, as recorded: 27.5 ms for a run that answers at once and 163.9 ms with an output schema, 40 to 280 ms for a fold worker's start, 103.5 s and 111.1 s for the rebuild of 100,000 events on SQLite and PostgreSQL, under load. The build measures what is on `main` and then itself on the same machine the same day, idle, with a load average below the cores, and records both in the tables of the computation README, the engine README and the host README: the first job of a worker, cold; the median of warm runs, plain and with a schema; a warm page of the example's 1,000 events beside the cold page it replaces and the JSON round trip of the page's bytes on the measuring thread; the rebuild on both stores with its pages and writes; and a job after the idle minute, cold again. What a warm job still pays is copying: a program's request is its input of at most 256 KiB and its output of at most 1 MiB as JSON text (`program-answer.ts:109,80`), a page's its events of at most 4 MiB and each view of at most 512 KiB in and out (`fold-answer.ts:80,98-104,110`), and two messages. The warm pool must reach these numbers, or the build is not done: a warm run of a program that answers at once costs at most the JSON round trip of its request measured on the thread plus 2 ms for its two messages, with or without an output schema, so the cold start's share is gone; a warm page of up to 1,000 events costs less than the cold page by at least the cold fold worker's start measured alone the same day, and a warm page costs at most 10 ms beyond its folds, measured at rest, since beyond the round trip of its bytes a page pays the passing of its two messages, the main thread's bookkeeping of the page and the worker's assembly of its answer, which a bound of the copied bytes alone left out; the rebuild on each store is faster than the cold one by at least the pages read times that start; and the first job of a worker starts in at most 200 ms.

### 7. What this record does not decide

The session worker's artifact cache and its loading of a session, which 0012 decides on this pool; transferring a job's values instead of copying them as JSON text, since the gates above are met with copies; starting workers before they are needed; a worker kept for one brain or one org; a process instead of a thread for memory a thread's limit does not see (0012, section 10); and workers for workflow expressions, which run on the main thread by design.

## Consequences

- A run of a computation or recall function, and a page of folds, cost their own work and the copying of their bytes: the thread's start is paid once per module and per minute of idleness, and the evaluator and the schema compiler are loaded once, which is what the computation README and the reference page promised would come next.
- The isolation decision 0005 bought with a thread for each run is kept by what the evaluator is, a pure function whose state lives in the call, and by letting go of a worker after any job the pool had to stop or whose answer says so, so nothing a bad job left behind reaches the next; the build proves it rather than assumes it.
- The permits stay the one bound, now on threads alive as well as jobs at once, so the memory an operator plans for does not change; two constants, a minute and a thousand jobs, are the session worker's too, and the pool's rule of keeping is the envelope's, so a new kind of worker brings its own.
- Five worker modules become one plain pair in the engine and one checked module in specs, none importing Node; two copies of the output check and four wordings of its issues become one each; two hand-written readers become schemas; two scripted pools become one; a lint rule keeps the port in the pool.
- 0005's amendment "One worker for each run" is superseded; the computation and recall reference pages, the container page and three READMEs describe the warm pool and its measurements.

## Amendments from the build

What building it showed, and what it changed in the decision above:

- **The job loop takes the checks and keeps two caches of them.** `serveJobs({ program, fold, checks })` takes the module's `checks`, an output check and a view check of a schema, and compiles each once for each schema's JSON text, in a cache for outputs and one for views of 262,144 characters each, since the same schema compiles to a different check for an output and for a view, whose issues name what they are about. Without `checks`, a program goes unchecked and a view that keeps a schema stalls, as the pool's own fold worker always did.
- **The loop can serve a port it is given, and the worker modules are loaded on the main thread by their tests.** A worker the pool terminates writes no coverage of its own, and a warm worker is always terminated, so the loop's tests serve a `MessageChannel` on the main thread, and the one-line worker modules are covered by loading them there, where there is no parent port and they serve nothing.
- **An idle worker is unreferenced as its timer is.** A pool whose workers are idle holds no process open, closed or not.
- **A request the loop cannot read is answered `unreadable`.** A page's job reads it as `unreadable`; a program's, which has no such answer, as a crash. Neither keeps the worker.
- **The first job of a plain worker is colder.** The job loop decodes each envelope with `effect`'s schemas, which the pool's program worker did not load before, so its first job pays for loading `effect` once; the checked worker loaded it already.
- **Writing through `__proto__` by index or by `setpath` answers `unfit`.** The evaluator answers a value it cannot carry as JSON so, as it did before; the literal answers, and no job after them finds `polluted`.
- **The threads alive are counted from the diagnostic report.** The test of the permits counts the workers `process.report.getReport()` lists, and names each by the thread id its test module answers.
- **The measurements, under load and at rest, and the bound of a warm page amended.** Measured on 2026-10-07 on the machine of the earlier figures, the code before and this pool alternately, twice, under a load average of 94 to 186 on its 16 cores from other work, not idle as section 5 asks (the engine README, _The workers of the pool, measured_, and the host README hold the tables). A warm run cost 0.07 to 0.43 ms, under the JSON round trip of its request, 0.004 ms, and 2 ms, with and without an output schema; the rebuild was faster on each store, in each round, by more than its pages times the start of a fold worker of the code before. A warm page cost 266 ms less than a cold one, more than a fold worker's start, but 61.6 ms more than its folds on the measuring thread, over the allowance then written, the round trip of its bytes, 0.8 ms, and 2 ms, and over the 10 ms that replaced it; and the first job of a worker took 115.6 ms in one round and 229.9 ms in the other, past 200 ms. Measured again later the same day with the machine at rest, at a load average of 1.9 to 3.7, the code before and this pool alternately: a warm run cost 0.07 to 0.09 ms, against 22 to 27 ms and 102 to 104 ms with an output schema for a worker started for each run, met; a warm page cost 149 to 162 ms less than a cold one, more than a fold worker's start alone, 100 to 103 ms, met; the first job of a worker took 100.7 and 104.1 ms, and 125.4 ms after a minute idle, within 200 ms, met; and the rebuild was 4.1 seconds faster on SQLite and 4.8 seconds on PostgreSQL, more than its 101 pages times the start of a fold worker of the code before at rest, 31 ms, about 3.1 seconds, met on both in one build each. A warm page cost beyond its folds 4.5, 1.0, 4.9, 4.9, 8.1 and −1.4 ms in six runs, a median of about 4.7 ms, on a page whose folds cost 281 to 290 ms on the measuring thread. The allowance first written in section 5, the round trip of its bytes and 2 ms, 2.6 to 2.7 ms here, was too tight: it was derived from the bytes copied alone and left out the passing of the two messages, the page's bookkeeping on the main thread and the worker's assembly of its answer, and the measurement's own spread across runs was wider than it. Section 5 now bounds a warm page at 10 ms beyond its folds, measured at rest, which every one of the six runs meets.
