# @on.auto/prediction

The implementation of prediction functions. A prediction function answers a question with numbers learned from the playbook's own history: a quantity over a grain of time, one or more periods ahead, for one series at a time, as a likely value with a low and a high. Keep the reusable function, the model a version of it holds, and the prediction a run returns distinct, as [Playbook terminology](../../docs/concepts/terminology.md) says. A model is a file in the ONNX format that a trainer builds outside the playbook from the rows the playbook keeps.

This package holds the function's document, its derived schemas, its checks at save, the dataset the playbook keeps for it, the files a build reads and the upload of a dataset; a build, from its refusals and its request to its version, the trainer's three routes, the scoring of the candidates against the baselines, the shadow and the activation of a version; the question the active version answers; and the model processes that load the models, with the files they read.

User documentation is [Prediction function format](../../docs/reference/prediction-format.md), published at [on.auto/docs](https://on.auto/docs/): the document, the fields, the dataset and its grammar, the brief, a build and the trainer's path, the signature of a candidate, its scoring, a question and the bounds; [HTTP API](../../docs/reference/http.md#prediction-functions) lists the five routes served over HTTP alone. Update them alongside behaviour changes; `src/document/reference-page.test.ts` checks that the page's example is the document the tests run, that its rows are taken by an upload, that its uploaded data part parses, and that its bounds are the ones `predictionBounds` holds, and `src/document/reference-trainer.test.ts` that the bounds of a build's trainer are the ones the code holds.

## The document

`parsePredictionDocument(source)` reads a document with the shared reader of [`@on.auto/definitions/document`](../../packages/definitions/README.md#reading-function-documents) and gives a `PredictionFunctionDefinitionDocument`: the optional `description`, `predicts`, `data`, `evaluation`, `source` and `rules`, the brief compiled as a Liquid template, the digest of the data part, `dataSha256`, the `data` expressions of the filters with their pointers and lines, and the derived schemas. The front matter's keys are `description`, `predicts`, `data`, `evaluation`, `source` and `rules`; every other key, `input` and `output` among them, is an issue at its line. Each part is checked in its own module (`src/document/predicts-part.ts`, `evaluation-part.ts`, `source-part.ts`, `src/data/data-part.ts`), and every problem is reported at once, each at the line of the value it names: a repeated horizon, a holdout or a window outside the bounds the horizons and the season set, `seasonal-naive` without a season, a `given` name that is not a feature, a feature or the target named `series` or `period`, a feature named as the target, a `time` or `series` pointing where none of the named event types holds a value, `count` with `of` or another aggregation without it, `to` beside `through`, a rule past its bounds. The brief (`src/brief`) reads `dataset` and `function` alone: a name outside them, a list or a mapping written where text goes, and a brief that renders past 64 KiB, or takes longer than the template engine's 200 ms to render, over the largest dataset the bounds admit (`src/brief/brief-dataset.ts`) are refused at their lines.

The digest of the data part, `dataSha256`, is the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of what the rows of its dataset depend on: the data part without its `window` and its `given`, with the grain beside it (`src/data/data-digest.ts`), since a row is folded from the filters, the pointers and the aggregations at the grain, and neither the window a build reads nor the features a question supplies changes one. Two functions whose rows are folded alike share one dataset, and an edit that widens the window or names other given features rebuilds nothing.

`summarize` answers the description, the derived schemas and the `details` the registry keeps with the definition, `{ data_sha256, data }`, `data` being what the digest covers, so the host's projector folds a dataset without this package's parser. The input schema is `oneOf` a question, `{ series, horizon, given }` with `series` text of 1 to 256 bytes, whose `maxLength` of 256 a run's own byte count completes, and `given` required when the document lists it and holding those names alone, and a build, `{ build: {} }`; the output schema is `oneOf` a prediction and a build's outcome (`src/document/derived-schemas.ts`).

The capability's `check` (`src/capability/prediction-check.ts`) reads the other definitions of the playbook through the `SavedDefinitions` port of `@on.auto/definitions`, which answers the active version of a definition with its schemas: an approver named under `evaluation.activate.approved` is an active interaction function whose input schema takes `{ function, version, card }` and whose output schema is an object whose one required property `decision` is `approve` or `reject`; a function named under `source.through` is an active interaction function whose input schema takes `{ build: {} }` and whose output schema is an object whose one required property `candidates` is a list of text (`src/capability/called-functions.ts`). A function that names an uploaded dataset is checked against what the port's `content(sha256)` answers (`src/capability/uploaded-datasets.ts`): the playbook keeps content under the digest, as `text/csv`, whose header and first row (`src/upload/kept-head.ts`) are of the grain `predicts.period` names and name `predicts.target` and then the features in `data.features`' order; each fault is refused at its line, so the standing never names a digest that was never uploaded. The filters' `data` expressions are then checked by the pool's check worker against `$data`, as a recall function's are.

## The dataset

`datasetFolder({ filters, rebuildsAtOnce, triesBeforeStall })` is a folder of the host's projector ([`@on.auto/workflow-host`](../../packages/workflow-host/README.md)), beside the recall views, over two tables it creates in the ledger's database: `prediction_datasets`, one row a dataset of a playbook, keyed by `playbook_key` and `data_sha256`, with its `phase`, `checkpoint`, `checkpoint_at`, `rows`, `series`, `first_period`, `last_period`, `events_folded`, `passed_over`, `tries`, the stall's `stalled_record` and `stalled_detail`, and the `data` part it folds; and `prediction_rows`, one row a series and period, keyed by `playbook_key`, `data_sha256`, `series` and `period_index`, the whole number that orders the periods of its grain, with the `period` in its ISO 8601 form, the `target` as `DOUBLE PRECISION`, the `target_count` and `target_at` that `mean` and `last` need, the `events` folded into it and the `features` as JSON, each feature as its value, count and time. A dataset is added when an active definition first names its data part, waits for one of `rebuildsAtOnce` slots, 2 a playbook, rebuilds from the first event of the history, goes live, and is dropped with its rows once no active definition names it.

The fold runs in the server's process over the page's decoded events (`src/dataset/dataset-fold.ts`): the filters are matched as a recall function's are, the `data` tests through the engine's filter sandbox, then for each event the time is read, in UTC, and its period found (`src/periods`: weeks from Monday as ISO 8601 numbers them, calendar quarters, days through the civil calendar), the series read, and the target and each feature merged into the row of that series and period with `count`, `sum`, `last` by the event's time, whatever order the events were recorded in, or `mean`; a missing or null value adds nothing. An event whose time is not a strict ISO 8601 time with its offset on a calendar day, a date alone among them, or names a leap second, whose series is not text of 1 to 256 bytes, or whose value is not a finite number, adds to no row: the dataset counts it in `passed_over` and the host notes `record_passed_over` with the reason `refused`, at most ten a page. A data test that raises passes its event over the same way, since the event caused it. One that runs out of time or memory meets a limit no event causes, as the engine treats a deadline or a memory stop, so it never passes its event over: the page ends before that event, the dataset's `tries` on its row count one more, and it rests at its checkpoint until the projector's next sweep, a second by default, which tries it again, as the views of recall functions are tried; a page that folds without a stop sets `tries` back to 0, and at `triesBeforeStall` tries, twenty, the bound the views use, the dataset stalls, phase `stalled`, its stall naming the event's record and the stop's words, and folds nothing more until no active definition names its data part. A page reads, of the rows it changes, only those of the series and periods its events touch, one indexed row a pair, so a page costs at most as many rows as it has events, 1,000, however many periods they span. A page's rows, its counts, its tries and its checkpoint are written in one transaction, which commits only if the checkpoint it read is still the row's.

`datasetsPortOn(database)` reads a dataset's row for `get_definition`'s `standing` (`src/capability/prediction-standing.ts`): `kind`, `data_sha256`, the phase, the checkpoint, the counts, the first and last period and `stall`, null until the dataset stalls and then `{ record, detail }`; a function that names an uploaded dataset stands as `{ kind: uploaded, sha256 }`.

## The files a build reads

`writtenDatasetFiles({ database, content, playbook, document, now })` writes the two files a build will read, through the content port, each kept under its SHA-256 (`src/files`): the scoring rows, the dense grid of the `window` periods that ends at the last period closed at `now`, and the training rows, the same without the last `holdout` periods. The header is `series,period`, the target and the features in the document's order; the rows are sorted by series, as their UTF-8 bytes sort, then by period, in CRLF records of RFC 4180; a period with no event is 0 for `count` and `sum` and empty for `last` and `mean`; a series observed in fewer than `holdout + 1` periods of the window is left out. It reads the dataset's row, the counts, the series and every row of both files in one snapshot of the host's database, `readConsistently`, so the two files are of the one checkpoint it names however far the fold moves on while they are written, and it never reads again. It refuses a dataset past 200,000 rows or 1,000 series, with the figures it counted, every series of the window observed often enough counted, or a file past 64 MiB, `too_large`, a dataset not yet live, not kept or stalled, `not_ready`, and a function that names an uploaded dataset, `uploaded`, each as a `DatasetFilesRefused` with its words, and a refusal keeps nothing: the scoring rows are written first, and the training rows are fewer, so the bytes can only be refused before anything is kept. A build calls it as it starts, and a shadow at each close it predicts at, through a content port of its own that writes the scoring rows into the model processes' rows cache. A function that names an uploaded dataset is read by `uploadedDatasetFiles` (`src/build/uploaded-files.ts`) instead: the upload kept under its digest, its last `window` periods up to its own last period the scoring rows and the same without the last `holdout` the training rows, in the same grammar, a period no row names empty, with no checkpoint.

## The upload

`uploadDataset(content)` is the operation `upload_dataset`, `PUT /v1/orgs/{org}/playbooks/{playbook}/datasets/{sha256}` under `playbook:write`, served over HTTP alone, so it is no tool on any MCP endpoint. Its body is `text/csv` with a `Content-Length` of at most 64 MiB; it is read as a stream (`src/upload`), checked as UTF-8 and against the grammar of the files above with any target and feature names, CRLF or LF records and a leading byte order mark allowed, and kept through the content port with the path's digest expected. A body that breaks the grammar is refused `invalid_input` with the line of its first fault, one whose digest is not the path's with both digests, the body's the one the port computed as it kept the stream, and nothing is kept then. The answer is the digest, the bytes, the rows, the series, the columns, the grain and the first and last period.

## A run

`makePredictionFunctionAdapter({ pool, datasets, ports, mostFunctions })` makes the capability, type `prediction`, media type `text/markdown`, guide `prediction-function`; the server gives it the computation pool, for the check, the host's `DatasetsPort`, and the `PredictionPorts` a build and a question use: the runtime's ledger with its content, the host's database for the consistent reads of the files, the model processes, the two file caches, the scoring permits, the bounds of a build (`buildsPerDay`, `buildsAtOnce`, `mostOpenRequests`) and the count of the playbook's open requests. Its `mostActive` is `mostFunctions`, 32 unless the server's `PREDICTION_MAX_FUNCTIONS` says otherwise. A run (`src/run`) refuses an input nested past 512 levels, one the question's or the build's schema refuses and a question whose series takes more than 256 bytes, `invalid_input` with the pointers, and is then a build, `{ build: {} }`, or a question. `finishesLaterFor` is false for a question, so a question's run cannot be cancelled from outside while its call is in flight, and true for anything else, and `longestRunFor` is 10 seconds for a question and, for a build or an input it cannot tell, the build's wait, `source.expires`, plus eight candidates scored at ten minutes each and a minute to start (`src/run/run-lengths.ts`). The capability declares, on a build's run, `dataset_assembled`, `candidate_uploaded`, `candidate_refused`, `candidate_scored`, `candidate_disqualified`, `baseline_scored` and `baseline_not_walked`, and the stream kind `models`, whose stream `models/<function>` holds `version_built`, `shadow_predicted`, `shadow_graded`, `shadow_passed`, `shadow_failed`, `version_activated` and `activation_refused` (`src/facts`); `record_passed_over` is a note of the host, not a fact.

## A build

A build's run (`src/build/build-run.ts`) refuses, as its first act and recording nothing more than its start and its rejection, a build while another of the function is open in the builds projection, `conflict`, `build_in_flight`, naming its run; the function's `buildsPerDay` builds over the trailing 24 hours, `unavailable`, `builds_full`, naming when it may ask again; `buildsAtOnce` builds in flight across every playbook of the server, counted on the projection's `builds_in_flight` index, `unavailable`, `builds_full`; and, for a build that asks through the inbox, a playbook with its `mostOpenRequests` open, `unavailable`, `requests_full` (`src/build/build-refusals.ts`). It then writes the two files, refused `unavailable`, `dataset_not_ready`, or `conflict`, `dataset_too_large`, with the figures of `writtenDatasetFiles`; records `dataset_assembled` with their digests, sizes and figures; renders the brief over them, `conflict`, `unworkable` past 64 KiB, and keeps it through the content port as `text/markdown`; and defers with its request record (`src/build/build-request.ts`). With `source.to` the record is the inbox's: `to`, a `message` within 8 KiB that names the run, its expiry, the three routes and how to answer, then the brief's opening, cut at a code point, the `answer_schema` `{ candidates, recipe?, limitations? }`, `expires_at`, `requested_at` and `build: { brief_sha256, brief_bytes, training_sha256, training_bytes, routes }`. With `source.through` it is `{ waiting_for: { run }, expires_at, build }`, `run` the id of 0011 derived from the build's run and `/request`, and the due work starts that child.

## The trainer's routes

`readBuildBrief`, `readBuildRows` and `uploadCandidate` (`src/trainer`) are the operations `read_build_brief`, `read_build_rows` and `upload_candidate`, `GET /runs/{run_id}/build/brief` and `/rows` under `playbook:read` and `POST /runs/{run_id}/build/candidates` under `playbook:write`, served over HTTP alone. Each loads the run's stream and answers only a build that waits for its answer (`src/trainer/waiting-builds.ts`): another run, or one of another type, is `conflict`, `not_a_build`, and a build that took its answer, whose request or child ended, or whose run ended, `conflict`, `build_ended`. The brief is the content its record names, as `text/markdown`; the rows the training rows, streamed from the content port as `text/csv`.

An upload's body is an `application/octet-stream` with a `Content-Length` of at most 64 MiB, which the HTTP layer requires before it reads the body. It is written as it streams into the models cache, its SHA-256 computed as it is written, and checked in a model process, a `check` job within 30 seconds (`src/trainer/candidate-checks.ts`): the file loads, its inputs and outputs are the signature the function feeds (`src/trainer/signature-check.ts`: `y` `[1, L]`, `x` `[1, L, F]` with features, `given` `[1, H, G]` with given features, `h` `[1]` of int64 with `accumulates`, and `q` `[1, H, 3]`, or `[1, 1, 3]` with `accumulates`, each float32 but `h`; the first axis 1 or dynamic, `L` any length or dynamic, `H` the longest horizon or dynamic, and dynamic with `accumulates`, `F` and `G` the counts the document names, and no other input or output) and its opset, read from the file's own operator sets (`src/process/onnx-opset.ts`), is 13 or later. A file refused records `candidate_refused { sha256, bytes, because, detail }`, `because` `failed_to_load`, `wrong_signature` or `too_many`, and answers `invalid_input`; a check that finds every model process taken answers `unavailable`, `model_process_busy`, recording nothing. A file accepted is staged under the build's run through the content port and recorded `candidate_uploaded { sha256, bytes, opset, inputs, outputs }` under a rule that refuses a ninth or a repeat, the record tried again up to three times while the run's stream changed meanwhile; the same bytes again answer the same digest and record nothing. The answer is `{ candidate, bytes, opset }`.

The answer to a build is `request_answered` on its run, by `answer_interaction` for an inbox request or, through a function, the `/request` child's `run_succeeded`. `buildAnswerIssues` (`src/capability/build-answers.ts`) is the capability's `answerIssues`: a digest no `candidate_uploaded` of the build carries is refused `invalid_input` at its pointer, and a `recipe` or `limitations` past 8 KiB at its own.

## The builds projection and its due work

`predictionBuilds` (`src/builds/builds-projection.ts`) is the keyed projection `prediction_builds`, version 1, over the run streams and the `models` streams, one row a build keyed by its run id, a child's facts keyed by the run that called it and a model fact by the build it names: the `function`, its `definition_version`, the `standing` (`starting_request`, `waiting_for_trainer`, `scoring`, `in_shadow`, `awaiting_approval`, `ended`), `through`, `requested_at`, `expires_at`, the candidates `uploaded` and `scored`, the `winner`, the `version`, the `rule`, the `shadow_origins` predicted, `next_due_at`, `open`, `in_flight`, set until the build's run ends, `ended`, `staged` and the `cause`, the message that last moved it. Its indexes are `by_open` and `by_requested` for the refusals and `builds_due` and `builds_in_flight` across playbooks.

`buildsDue({ ports, runs })` (`src/builds/builds-due.ts`) is the host's due work in the lane that does not call out: each row whose `next_due_at` has passed is performed, and each perform reads the build's run, its facts and the function's models and does one thing, recording one fact or changing one row (`src/builds/run-steps.ts`, `run-ended.ts`). While the run waits: a build through a function starts its child once under the derived id, through `runs`, with `calledBy: { runId, reference: '/request', run: 1 }`, and a child refused, or one that ends without an answer, ends the build `rejected`, reason `unanswered`, with the child's kind or `undelivered`; an inbox build nobody answers is ended by the playbook's requests at its `expires_at`, `rejected`, reason `unanswered`, kind `expired`, which closes its row; an answered build scores its next unscored candidate, then records the baselines, then ends. A perform that finds no scoring permit free, or every model process taken, rests the row a minute. Once the run has ended, however it ended, the candidates staged under it are unstaged, a child still in flight, as a build cancelled through `cancel_run` leaves it, is cancelled, and a build that succeeded with a version runs its shadow and its activation. A restart carries on from the last fact recorded, since every perform reads what was recorded rather than what it remembers, and servers sharing a database perform the work of the one that holds the host's lease.

## Scoring

A candidate's scoring (`src/scoring/scoring-step.ts`) takes one of `buildsAtOnce` scoring permits, puts the staged file in the models cache and the scoring rows in the rows cache, and walks the candidate in one `walk` job within 10 minutes: the child reads the rows and, for each series and each of the `holdout` origins, feeds the window up to the origin and answers its `q` (`src/process/walk-job.ts`). The server (`src/scoring/candidate-scoring.ts`, `metrics.ts`) reads each step the holdout holds and scores it per horizon: `mase`, the absolute error over the series' in-sample naive error, one period or one season back; `winkler` at 80%, the interval's width plus ten times the distance of a truth outside it; `quantile`, the pinball loss of the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles; and the coverage. The baselines `persistence` and `seasonal-naive` are forecast by the playbook's own code over the same origins (`src/scoring/baselines.ts`), each with an interval from its own errors over the training periods, and recorded as `baseline_scored` with their scores and the series they walked, or `baseline_not_walked` when no series is long enough. Against each baseline the moving-block bootstrap (`src/scoring/bootstrap.ts`) resamples the differences of the primary metric at each origin 1,000 times, in blocks of the season or the cube root of the origins, from a seed of the run id's SHA-256, and gives `p_better`, the share of resamples whose mean favours the candidate. A candidate is `candidate_disqualified` with its `because`, `timed_out`, `out_of_memory`, `crashed`, `not_loaded`, `answered_badly`, `coverage_below_floor` or `not_better`, or `candidate_scored` with its scores, coverage, `p_better` and duration. The winner is the scored candidate with the lowest mean of the primary metric over the horizons, the first answered on a tie.

With `accumulates`, each origin is the end of a period and the quantity the total to the end of its enclosing period, fed `h`, the periods left; `persistence` is the run-rate, the mean of the enclosing period so far times the periods left, `seasonal-naive` the same periods' sum one season earlier, and `mase` is scaled by the run-rate's error over the training periods (`src/scoring/accumulated-baselines.ts`).

## The ending of a build

Once every candidate is scored and every baseline recorded (`src/scoring/ending-steps.ts`), a winner is kept as the next version: `version_built` on `models/<function>`, decided by `modelsDecider` (`src/models/model-decider.ts`) once a build, with the version's card (`src/scoring/version-card.ts`): the build, who trained it, the dataset's digests and figures, the file's digest, size and opset, the inputs, the features, the scores, the coverage, `p_better` against each baseline, the baselines' figures and the rule, at most 4 KiB as JSON, the features kept as content when the card would pass it, and the answer's `recipe` and `limitations` kept as content and named by their digests. The run then succeeds with `{ version, winner, candidates }`, each candidate with its verdict, as `playbook:<id>`; `version` and `winner` are null when none won, and the build ends there.

## Shadow and activation

A build with a version moves to its shadow (`src/shadow`). The first version of a function, a function over an uploaded dataset and `shadow_periods: 0` pass at once, `shadow_passed` with no figures. Otherwise, at each of the `shadow_periods` closes of the grain after the version was built, of the enclosing period with `accumulates` (`src/shadow/shadow-schedule.ts`), the due work writes the dataset's scoring rows at that close into the rows cache and walks the new version and the active one from that origin, one `shadow_predicted` a version, its predictions kept as content; once a predicted period closes, it grades both against the dataset's rows, `shadow_graded` with each metric and the series with no truth counted apart (`src/shadow/shadow-grades.ts`); and once every grade is in, `shadow_passed` or `shadow_failed` with the two versions' figures of the primary metric, the new version passing when it is no worse. Between closes the row rests until the next.

A version that passed activates by its rule (`src/shadow/model-lifecycle.ts`): `automatic` records `version_activated { rule: automatic }`; `{ approved: <function> }` starts the `/approval` child with `{ function, version, card }` and records `version_activated { rule: approved, approved_by }` when it answers `approve`, or `activation_refused { because: approver_rejected }`, `unanswered` when it ends unanswered; a version that failed its shadow records `activation_refused { because: shadow_failed }`. `activateVersion` (`src/models/activate-version.ts`) is the operation `activate_version`, `POST /definitions/prediction/{name}/activate` under `playbook:write` over HTTP alone, which records `version_activated { rule: requested }` for a built version whatever its shadow decided, `not_found` for a version not built and `conflict`, `already_active` for the active one; a requested activation of a version whose build had not settled settles it.

`predictionModels` (`src/models/models-projection.ts`) is the keyed projection `prediction_models`, version 1, over the `models` streams, one row a function: the `active` version with its data and file digests, the `latest`, how many `versions`, the `challenger` in its shadow with its build, the `shadow_predicted` and `shadow_graded` counts and the `shadow` result. `get_definition`'s standing (`src/capability/prediction-standing.ts`, `model-standing.ts`) adds `models`, read from it, and `build`, the open build of the builds projection with its standing, `requested_at`, `expires_at` and its candidates uploaded and scored, beside the dataset.

## A question

A question's run (`src/question`) reads the function's row of the models projection and refuses `conflict`, `no_active_version`, before a version is active, and `data_changed` once the document's `dataSha256` is not the active version's; reads the series' window at the dataset's checkpoint, the last `L` periods up to the last closed one, or up to an uploaded dataset's last period, refusing a series the window does not hold `invalid_input` and a dataset not live `unavailable`, `dataset_not_ready` (`src/question/series-window.ts`); feeds `y`, `x`, `given`, its values repeated over the steps ahead, and with `accumulates` `h`, the periods left in the enclosing period; and runs the active version's file, from the models cache, in a `run` job within 10 seconds, on a process that holds it when one does. It reads the asked step's three numbers from `q` and succeeds with the prediction and the record `{ version, origin, dataset_checkpoint, duration_ms }`. Every process taken answers `unavailable`, `model_process_busy`; a process that dies, runs out of memory, or answers a number that is not finite, which the process answers as a failed job so that the pool lets it go, answers `unavailable`, `model_process_failed`, as does an answer of another shape.

## The model processes

`modelPorts({ pool, scoringAtOnce })` (`src/process/model-ports.ts`) gives the model processes, the two file caches and the scoring permits from the server's process pool. `modelProcesses` sends each job to `modelProcessModule`, `src/process/model-process.ts`, which serves the engine's process loop with the jobs of `src/process/model-jobs.ts` over `onnxruntime-node`, pinned at 1.30.0: `check` loads a file and answers its signature and opset, `run` answers a question's `q`, and `walk` answers every origin's `q` of every series of a rows file. Their deadlines are `modelDeadlines`, 30 seconds, 10 seconds and 10 minutes, at which the pool kills the child. A job is keyed by the model's digest, so a process that loaded a model serves its next job; the child keeps one session a file, its intra-op threads the larger of 1 and the cores divided by the processes (`threadsOf`), and answers an allocation the runtime could not make as the process loop's memory stop. `walk` jobs are long jobs of the pool, at most `PREDICTION_BUILDS_AT_ONCE` of its processes, so a question always finds one.

`fileCache` (`src/process/model-files.ts`) keeps the files a model process reads under `/var/tmp/auto-runtime`, the models in `models` and the rows in `rows`, at most 1 GiB each: a file is written under a name of its own as it streams, its digest computed as it is written, renamed to `<sha256>.onnx` or `<sha256>.csv` once whole, and the files used least recently removed past the bound, never the one just written; a file the cache does not hold, or holds no longer, is written again from the content port, and one whose bytes are not its digest is removed and refused.

## Bounds

`predictionBounds` holds them: 1 to 8 horizons of 1 to 52 periods, a season of 2 to 366, a holdout of at least 8 and a window of at most 1,000, 8 filters, 40 features, names and the unit of 1 to 64 characters, 32 rules of 256 bytes, shares from 0.5 to 0.99, a shadow of 0 to 52 periods, a build's wait from one minute to 30 days, a series of 1 to 256 bytes, a dataset of 200,000 rows over 1,000 series in 64 MiB, a brief of 64 KiB rendered within the template engine's 200 ms, 10 seconds a question, 8 candidates of 64 MiB a build, 10 minutes a candidate's scoring and a minute to start a build, two rebuilds at once a playbook, ten notes a page and twenty tries before a dataset stalls; `modelDeadlines` holds the 30 seconds of a check, `mostNoteBytes` the 8 KiB of a recipe and of a limitation, `mostCardBytes` the 4 KiB of a card and `mostCachedBytes` the 1 GiB of each cache. The server reads `buildsPerDay` from `PREDICTION_BUILDS_PER_DAY`, 1 to 100, 4 by default, and `buildsAtOnce` from `PREDICTION_BUILDS_AT_ONCE`. Each has a test that reaches it here, and the server's `src/prediction` reaches the upload's, the playbook's, the build's and the routes' over HTTP.

## Testing

`@on.auto/prediction/testing` exports `weeklyOrders`, the example document, `weeklyOrdersLines` and `weeklyOrdersWith(...replaced)`, which changes its lines, `uploadedWeeklyOrders(sha256)`, the example over an uploaded dataset, the daily documents and rows the build tests use (`dailyVisits`, `dailyTotals`, `dailyRows`, `steadyVisits`, each `...With` and `uploaded...`), and `testPorts`, the `PredictionPorts` over a memory ledger with both projections, a real process pool closed after the test and caches in a scratch folder, its module, deadlines, processes and bounds the test's to choose. The dataset's tests run on SQLite and, with `LEDGER_TEST_POSTGRESQL_URL` set, on PostgreSQL, each on a database of its own, through the suites in `src/dataset-testing`, which drive the host's projector with `projectedFolders` of `@on.auto/workflow-host/testing`; the builds and the questions over a projected dataset run the same way through the suites in `src/build-testing`. The run's tests use a fake datasets port and a fake `SavedDefinitions` that keeps content too (`src/testing/prediction-runs.ts`), and `src/testing/cancels-in-a-call.ts` cancels a run from outside while its call is in flight, through `cancel_run`, just before its call records its end.

A build's tests drive a playbook of the operations' testing harness (`src/build-testing/build-playbook.ts`, `playbook-calls.ts`) and the due work by hand, one perform at a time or until nothing is due (`due-driving.ts`), with the candidates of `candidates/`: small ONNX models, each a graph whose answer the test knows, among them a mean, a last value, an interval too narrow or too wide, a model that answers `NaN`, whole numbers or a sequence, one with an input the function does not feed, one that allocates 16 GiB, one whose tensor reads its data from a file outside its own folder, one of opset 12, a running total and a file that is not a model. `pnpm --filter @on.auto/prediction candidates` writes them again from `candidates/build-candidates.ts`, through the writer of ONNX's protobuf form in `candidates/onnx-writer.ts`, byte for byte the same. The tests that score, check and answer run the real model process; those that must not spend a deadline, or a process that ends, sleeps, allocates or answers what a model does not, run the scripted model process of `src/build-testing/scripted-models.ts`, a module written to a scratch folder that serves the real jobs or the next script a test names. The child's own module is tested in the test process, since the pool's children are ended with `SIGKILL`, which writes no coverage.

## Source

`src/index.ts` is the entry point and `src/testing/index.ts` the entry point of the test support. `src/document` holds the document: its type, the front matter, the parts and parsing, and the derived schemas. `src/data` holds the data part, its filters, pointers, measures and digest, and `src/brief` the brief's variables, template and largest dataset. `src/periods` holds the calendar: civil days, grains, enclosing periods and the moments an event's time is read as. `src/dataset` holds the folder, its tables and statements, the fold and the port; `src/files` the files a build reads; `src/upload` the upload's operation, its CSV reader and grammar, and the reading of a kept dataset's head. `src/capability` holds the capability, its ports, its bounds, its checks of the functions it calls, of an uploaded dataset and of a build's answer, its standing, its words and the figures they print, and its guide, the public reference page, served to agents as `prediction-function`. `src/run` holds a run, its two kinds and its lengths; `src/build` a build's start, its refusals, its files, its brief and its request; `src/trainer` the trainer's routes and a candidate's checks; `src/facts` the facts of a build and of the models; `src/builds` the builds projection, its due work and a build's state; `src/scoring` the scoring grid, the metrics, the baselines, the bootstrap, the verdicts, the ending and the card; `src/models` the models stream's decider, its state, its projection and the activation operation; `src/shadow` the shadow's schedule, predictions, truths, grades and the activation that follows; `src/question` a question; and `src/process` the model processes, their module and jobs, the sessions, the feeds, the opset, the caches and the permits. `src/dataset-testing` and `src/build-testing` hold the suites and the harness the tests share, and `candidates/` the candidate models and their writer.
