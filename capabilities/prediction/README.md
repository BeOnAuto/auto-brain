# @on.auto/prediction

The implementation of prediction functions. A prediction function answers a question with numbers learned from the playbook's own history: a quantity over a grain of time, one or more periods ahead, for one series at a time, as a likely value with a low and a high. Keep the reusable function, the model a version of it holds, and the prediction a run returns distinct, as [Playbook terminology](../../docs/concepts/terminology.md) says. A model is a file in the ONNX format that a trainer builds outside the playbook from the rows the playbook keeps.

This package holds the function's document, its derived schemas, its checks at save, the dataset the playbook keeps for it, the two files a build reads and the upload of a dataset. It does not build, score or answer yet: a question is refused `conflict` with the kind `no_active_version`, and a build is refused `unavailable`, since this runtime offers no builds.

User documentation is [Prediction function format](../../docs/reference/prediction-format.md), published at [on.auto/docs](https://on.auto/docs/): the document, the fields, the dataset and its grammar, the brief and the bounds. Update it alongside behaviour changes; `src/document/reference-page.test.ts` checks that its example is the document the tests run, that its rows are taken by an upload, that its uploaded data part parses, and that its bounds are the ones `predictionBounds` holds.

## The document

`parsePredictionDocument(source)` reads a document with the shared reader of [`@on.auto/definitions/document`](../../packages/definitions/README.md#reading-function-documents) and gives a `PredictionFunctionDefinitionDocument`: the optional `description`, `predicts`, `data`, `evaluation`, `source` and `rules`, the brief compiled as a Liquid template, the digest of the data part, `dataSha256`, the `data` expressions of the filters with their pointers and lines, and the derived schemas. The front matter's keys are `description`, `predicts`, `data`, `evaluation`, `source` and `rules`; every other key, `input` and `output` among them, is an issue at its line. Each part is checked in its own module (`src/document/predicts-part.ts`, `evaluation-part.ts`, `source-part.ts`, `src/data/data-part.ts`), and every problem is reported at once, each at the line of the value it names: a repeated horizon, a holdout or a window outside the bounds the horizons and the season set, `seasonal-naive` without a season, a `given` name that is not a feature, a feature or the target named `series` or `period`, a feature named as the target, a `time` or `series` pointing where none of the named event types holds a value, `count` with `of` or another aggregation without it, `to` beside `through`, a rule past its bounds. The brief (`src/brief`) reads `dataset` and `function` alone: a name outside them, a list or a mapping written where text goes, and a brief that renders past 64 KiB over the largest dataset the bounds admit (`src/brief/brief-dataset.ts`) are refused at their lines.

The digest of the data part, `dataSha256`, is the SHA-256 of its canonical JSON with the grain beside it (`src/data/data-digest.ts`), since the rows of a dataset depend on the period as much as on the filters; two functions with the same data part and grain share one dataset.

`summarize` answers the description, the derived schemas and the `details` the registry keeps with the definition, `{ data_sha256, data }`, so the host's projector folds a dataset without this package's parser. The input schema is `oneOf` a question, `{ series, horizon, given }` with `given` required when the document lists it and holding those names alone, and a build, `{ build: {} }`; the output schema is `oneOf` a prediction and a build's outcome (`src/document/derived-schemas.ts`).

The capability's `check` (`src/capability/prediction-check.ts`) reads the other definitions of the playbook through the `SavedDefinitions` port of `@on.auto/definitions`, which answers the active version of a definition with its schemas: an approver named under `evaluation.activate.approved` is an active interaction function whose input schema takes `{ function, version, card }` and whose output schema is an object whose one required property `decision` is `approve` or `reject`; a function named under `source.through` is an active interaction function whose input schema takes `{ build: {} }` and whose output schema takes `candidates`, a list of text (`src/capability/called-functions.ts`). The filters' `data` expressions are then checked by the pool's check worker against `$data`, as a recall function's are.

## The dataset

`datasetFolder({ filters, rebuildsAtOnce })` is a folder of the host's projector ([`@on.auto/workflow-host`](../../packages/workflow-host/README.md)), beside the recall views, over two tables it creates in the ledger's database: `prediction_datasets`, one row a dataset of a playbook, keyed by `playbook_key` and `data_sha256`, with its `phase`, `checkpoint`, `checkpoint_at`, `rows`, `series`, `first_period`, `last_period`, `events_folded`, `passed_over` and the `data` part it folds; and `prediction_rows`, one row a series and period, keyed by `playbook_key`, `data_sha256`, `series` and `period_index`, the whole number that orders the periods of its grain, with the `period` in its ISO 8601 form, the `target` as `DOUBLE PRECISION`, the `target_count` and `target_at` that `mean` and `last` need, the `events` folded into it and the `features` as JSON, each feature as its value, count and time. A dataset is added when an active definition first names its data part, waits for one of `rebuildsAtOnce` slots, 2 a playbook, rebuilds from the first event of the history, goes live, and is dropped with its rows once no active definition names it.

The fold runs in the server's process over the page's decoded events (`src/dataset/dataset-fold.ts`): the filters are matched as a recall function's are, the `data` tests through the engine's filter sandbox, then for each event the time is read, in UTC, and its period found (`src/periods`: weeks from Monday as ISO 8601 numbers them, calendar quarters, days through the civil calendar), the series read, and the target and each feature merged into the row of that series and period with `count`, `sum`, `last` by the event's time, whatever order the events were recorded in, or `mean`; a missing or null value adds nothing. An event whose time is not a strict ISO 8601 time with its offset on a calendar day, or names a leap second, whose series is not text of 1 to 256 bytes, or whose value is not a finite number, adds to no row: the dataset counts it in `passed_over` and the host notes `record_passed_over` with the reason `refused`, at most ten a page. A data test that raises passes its event over the same way; one that runs out of time or memory ends the page before that event, and the dataset rests at its checkpoint until a later pass, until it has stopped at the same event `stopsBeforePassingOver` times in a row, three, counted in the server's memory, when that event is passed over and noted too, so no event holds a dataset back for good. A page's rows, its counts and its checkpoint are written in one transaction, which commits only if the checkpoint it read is still the row's.

`datasetsPortOn(database)` reads a dataset's row for `get_definition`'s `standing` (`src/capability/prediction-standing.ts`): `kind`, `data_sha256`, the phase, the checkpoint, the counts and the first and last period; a function that names an uploaded dataset stands as `{ kind: uploaded, sha256 }`.

## The files a build reads

`writtenDatasetFiles({ database, content, playbook, document, now })` writes the two files a build will read, through the content port, each kept under its SHA-256 (`src/files`): the scoring rows, the dense grid of the `window` periods that ends at the last period closed at `now`, and the training rows, the same without the last `holdout` periods. The header is `series,period`, the target and the features in the document's order; the rows are sorted by series, as their UTF-8 bytes sort, then by period, in CRLF records of RFC 4180; a period with no event is 0 for `count` and `sum` and empty for `last` and `mean`; a series observed in fewer than `holdout + 1` periods of the window is left out. It refuses a dataset past 200,000 rows or 1,000 series or a file past 64 MiB, `too_large`, a dataset not yet live or not kept, `not_ready`, and a function that names an uploaded dataset, `uploaded`, each as a `DatasetFilesRefused` with its words; when the dataset folds on while it is read, it reads again, three times at most, and is then refused `changing`. No build calls it yet.

## The upload

`uploadDataset(content)` is the operation `upload_dataset`, `PUT /v1/orgs/{org}/playbooks/{playbook}/datasets/{sha256}` under `playbook:write`, served over HTTP alone, so it is no tool on any MCP endpoint. Its body is `text/csv` with a `Content-Length` of at most 64 MiB; it is read as a stream (`src/upload`), checked as UTF-8 and against the grammar of the files above with any target and feature names, CRLF or LF records and a leading byte order mark allowed, and kept through the content port with the path's digest expected. A body that breaks the grammar is refused `invalid_input` with the line of its first fault, one whose digest is not the path's with both digests, and nothing is kept then. The answer is the digest, the bytes, the rows, the series, the columns, the grain and the first and last period.

## A run

`makePredictionFunctionAdapter({ pool, datasets, mostFunctions })` makes the capability, type `prediction`, media type `text/markdown`, guide `prediction-function`; the server gives it the computation pool, for the check, and the host's `DatasetsPort`. Its `mostActive` is `mostFunctions`, 32 unless the server's `PREDICTION_MAX_FUNCTIONS` says otherwise. A run (`src/run/prediction-run.ts`) refuses an input nested past 512 levels and one the question's or the build's schema refuses, `invalid_input` with the schema's pointers; then a question is refused `conflict`, `no_active_version`, and a build `unavailable`. `finishesLaterFor` is false for a question and true for anything else, and `longestRunFor` is 10 seconds for a question and, for a build or an input it cannot tell, the build's wait, `source.expires`, plus eight candidates scored at ten minutes each and a minute to start (`src/run/run-lengths.ts`). The capability declares no facts and no stream kinds of its own: `record_passed_over` is a note of the host, not a fact.

## Bounds

`predictionBounds` holds them: 1 to 8 horizons of 1 to 52 periods, a season of 2 to 366, a holdout of at least 8 and a window of at most 1,000, 8 filters, 40 features, names and the unit of 1 to 64 characters, 32 rules of 256 bytes, shares from 0.5 to 0.99, a shadow of 0 to 52 periods, a build's wait from one minute to 30 days, a series of 1 to 256 bytes, a dataset of 200,000 rows over 1,000 series in 64 MiB, a brief of 64 KiB, 10 seconds a question, two rebuilds at once a playbook, ten notes a page and three stops in a row before an event is passed over. Each has a test that reaches it here, and the server's `src/prediction` reaches the upload's and the playbook's over HTTP.

## Measurements

`pnpm --filter @on.auto/prediction measure` measures, outside the tests (`measure.ts`, with its parts in `measure/`), on SQLite and, with `LEDGER_MEASURE_POSTGRESQL_URL` set, on PostgreSQL: 200,000 events of 1,000 series over 200 days, one an order a series a day, folded by the projector into the dataset of a daily function with two features, then 10,000 more appended while it is live, and the two files of a build's start written through the ledger's own content store and read back. Measured on 2026-10-12 on Node 26.10.0 on an Apple M4 Max, 16 cores, at a load average of about 3, PostgreSQL 18.4 on the same machine, an x86_64 build run under translation:

| What                                                           | SQLite                         | PostgreSQL                     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| 200,000 events recorded, in 200 appends of 1,000               | 7.7 s                          | 6.9 s                          |
| the dataset rebuilt from them, in 201 pages, to 200,000 rows   | 8.9 s, 22,560 events a second  | 12.7 s, 15,803 events a second |
| 10,000 more events, in 10 appends, folded live from the first  | 0.92 s, 10,875 events a second | 0.63 s, 15,936 events a second |
| the two files over 200,000 rows written at a build's start     | 1.17 s                         | 1.26 s                         |
| the training rows, 192,000 of them, 5,606,039 bytes, read back | 4.3 ms, its digest checked     | 15.4 ms, its digest checked    |
| the scoring rows, 200,000 of them, 6,028,039 bytes, read back  | 3.6 ms, its digest checked     | 15.6 ms, its digest checked    |

The live figure counts from the first append and includes the ten appends, which at the recording's rate take about 0.39 s on SQLite and 0.35 s on PostgreSQL. The server's `src/prediction/dataset-upload-over-http.test.ts` uploads a dataset of 67,000,182 bytes, 200,000 rows over 1,000 series and 41 columns, through HTTP on both stores and reads it back whole.

## Testing

`@on.auto/prediction/testing` exports `weeklyOrders`, the example document, `weeklyOrdersLines` and `weeklyOrdersWith(...replaced)`, which changes its lines, and `uploadedWeeklyOrders(sha256)`, the example over an uploaded dataset. The dataset's tests run on SQLite and, with `LEDGER_TEST_POSTGRESQL_URL` set, on PostgreSQL, each on a database of its own, through the suites in `src/dataset-testing`, which drive the host's projector with `projectedFolders` of `@on.auto/workflow-host/testing`; the run's tests use a fake datasets port and a fake `SavedDefinitions` (`src/testing/prediction-runs.ts`).

## Source

`src/index.ts` is the entry point and `src/testing/index.ts` the entry point of the test support. `src/document` holds the document: its type, the front matter, the parts and parsing, and the derived schemas. `src/data` holds the data part, its filters, pointers, measures and digest, and `src/brief` the brief's variables, template and largest dataset. `src/periods` holds the calendar: civil days, grains and the moments an event's time is read as. `src/dataset` holds the folder, its tables and statements, the fold and the port; `src/files` the files a build reads; `src/upload` the upload's operation, its CSV reader and grammar. `src/capability` holds the capability, its bounds, check, standing and words, and its guide, the public reference page, served to agents as `prediction-function`. `src/run` holds a run and its lengths. `src/dataset-testing` holds the dataset's suites and what they share.
