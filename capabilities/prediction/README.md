# @on.auto/prediction

The implementation of prediction functions. A prediction function answers a question with numbers learned from the playbook's own history: a quantity over a grain of time, one or more periods ahead, for one series at a time, as a likely value with a low and a high. Keep the reusable function, the model a version of it holds, and the prediction a run returns distinct, as [Playbook terminology](../../docs/concepts/terminology.md) says. A model is a file in the ONNX format that a trainer builds outside the playbook from the rows the playbook keeps.

This package holds the function's document, its derived schemas, its checks at save, the dataset the playbook keeps for it, the two files a build reads and the upload of a dataset. It does not build, score or answer yet: a question is refused `conflict` with the kind `no_active_version`, and a build is refused `unavailable` with the kind `build_not_offered`, which answers without `Retry-After`, since this runtime offers no builds.

User documentation is [Prediction function format](../../docs/reference/prediction-format.md), published at [on.auto/docs](https://on.auto/docs/): the document, the fields, the dataset and its grammar, the brief and the bounds. Update it alongside behaviour changes; `src/document/reference-page.test.ts` checks that its example is the document the tests run, that its rows are taken by an upload, that its uploaded data part parses, and that its bounds are the ones `predictionBounds` holds.

## The document

`parsePredictionDocument(source)` reads a document with the shared reader of [`@on.auto/definitions/document`](../../packages/definitions/README.md#reading-function-documents) and gives a `PredictionFunctionDefinitionDocument`: the optional `description`, `predicts`, `data`, `evaluation`, `source` and `rules`, the brief compiled as a Liquid template, the digest of the data part, `dataSha256`, the `data` expressions of the filters with their pointers and lines, and the derived schemas. The front matter's keys are `description`, `predicts`, `data`, `evaluation`, `source` and `rules`; every other key, `input` and `output` among them, is an issue at its line. Each part is checked in its own module (`src/document/predicts-part.ts`, `evaluation-part.ts`, `source-part.ts`, `src/data/data-part.ts`), and every problem is reported at once, each at the line of the value it names: a repeated horizon, a holdout or a window outside the bounds the horizons and the season set, `seasonal-naive` without a season, a `given` name that is not a feature, a feature or the target named `series` or `period`, a feature named as the target, a `time` or `series` pointing where none of the named event types holds a value, `count` with `of` or another aggregation without it, `to` beside `through`, a rule past its bounds. The brief (`src/brief`) reads `dataset` and `function` alone: parsing compiles it and refuses a brief that does not compile and a name outside them at their lines; the compile is cheap and deterministic, held by the template engine's bounds on characters and names and by no clock, 0.11 ms for the example's brief and 4.7 ms for one of 65,535 characters at the median, so every `prepare` of a run pays it. Rendering is the check's, below, since its 200 ms bound is a clock's.

The digest of the data part, `dataSha256`, is the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of what the rows of its dataset depend on: the data part without its `window` and its `given`, with the grain beside it (`src/data/data-digest.ts`), since a row is folded from the filters, the pointers and the aggregations at the grain, and neither the window a build reads nor the features a question supplies changes one. Two functions whose rows are folded alike share one dataset, and an edit that widens the window or names other given features rebuilds nothing.

`summarize` answers the description, the derived schemas and the `details` the registry keeps with the definition, `{ data_sha256, data }`, `data` being what the digest covers, so the host's projector folds a dataset without this package's parser. The input schema is `oneOf` a question, `{ series, horizon, given }` with `series` text of 1 to 256 bytes, whose `maxLength` of 256 a run's own byte count completes, and `given` required when the document lists it and holding those names alone, and a build, `{ build: {} }`; the output schema is `oneOf` a prediction and a build's outcome (`src/document/derived-schemas.ts`).

The capability's `check` (`src/capability/prediction-check.ts`), which runs at a save and never at a run, renders the brief over the largest dataset the bounds admit (`src/brief/brief-dataset.ts`) and refuses, at its line, a brief that writes a list or a mapping where text goes, reads an item the largest dataset does not have, renders past 64 KiB or takes longer than the template engine's 200 ms to render. It reads the other definitions of the playbook through the `SavedDefinitions` port of `@on.auto/definitions`, which answers the active version of a definition with its schemas: an approver named under `evaluation.activate.approved` is an active interaction function whose input schema takes `{ function, version, card }` and whose output schema is an object whose one required property `decision` is `approve` or `reject`; a function named under `source.through` is an active interaction function whose input schema takes `{ build: {} }` and whose output schema is an object whose one required property `candidates` is a list of text (`src/capability/called-functions.ts`). A function that names an uploaded dataset is checked against what the port's `content(sha256)` answers (`src/capability/uploaded-datasets.ts`): the playbook keeps content under the digest, as `text/csv`, whose header and first row (`src/upload/kept-head.ts`) are of the grain `predicts.period` names and name `predicts.target` and then the features in `data.features`' order; each fault is refused at its line, so the standing never names a digest that was never uploaded. The filters' `data` expressions are then checked by the pool's check worker against `$data`, as a recall function's are.

## The dataset

`datasetFolder({ filters, triesBeforeStall, rebuildsAtOnce })` is a folder of the host's projector ([`@on.auto/workflow-host`](../../packages/workflow-host/README.md)), beside the recall views, over two tables it creates in the ledger's database: `prediction_datasets`, one row a dataset of a playbook, keyed by `playbook_key` and `data_sha256`, with its `phase`, `checkpoint`, `checkpoint_at`, `rows`, `series`, `first_period`, `last_period`, `events_folded`, `passed_over`, `tries`, the stall's `stalled_record` and `stalled_detail`, and the `data` part it folds; and `prediction_rows`, one row a series and period, keyed by `playbook_key`, `data_sha256`, `series` and `period_index`, the whole number that orders the periods of its grain, with the `period` in its ISO 8601 form, the `target` as `DOUBLE PRECISION`, the `target_count` and `target_at` that `mean` and `last` need, the `events` folded into it and the `features` as JSON, each feature as its value, count and time. A dataset is added when an active definition first names its data part, waits for one of `rebuildsAtOnce` slots, 2 a playbook, rebuilds from the first event of the history, goes live, and is dropped with its rows once no active definition names it.

The fold runs in the server's process over the page's decoded events (`src/dataset/dataset-fold.ts`): the filters are matched as a recall function's are, the `data` tests through the engine's filter sandbox, which in the server is the pool's one filter thread, shared with the workflows' trigger and listener filters. A dataset's tested filters are prepared once a page, in one session of the sandbox (`filterTestingOf` of `@on.auto/workflow-engine`), and each event of the page is tested in it, each filter's `type` matched before its test, so an event meets the tests of its own type's filters alone (`src/dataset/dataset-matching.ts`); a test the sandbox stops lets that session go, the event's other filters are tested in sessions of their own, and the next event takes a fresh one. Then for each event the time is read, in UTC, and its period found (`src/periods`: weeks from Monday as ISO 8601 numbers them, calendar quarters, days through the civil calendar), the series read, and the target and each feature merged into the row of that series and period with `count`, `sum`, `last` by the event's time, whatever order the events were recorded in, or `mean`; a missing or null value adds nothing. An event whose time is not a strict ISO 8601 time with its offset on a calendar day, a date alone among them, or names a leap second, whose series is not text of 1 to 256 bytes, or whose value is not a finite number, adds to no row: the dataset counts it in `passed_over` and the host notes `record_passed_over` with the reason `refused`, at most ten a dataset a page. A data test that raises passes its event over the same way, since the event caused it. One that runs out of time or memory meets a limit no event causes, as the engine treats a deadline or a memory stop, so it never passes its event over: the page ends before that event, the dataset's `tries` on its row count one more, and it rests at its checkpoint until the projector's next sweep, a second by default, which tries it again, as the views of recall functions are tried; a page that folds without a stop sets `tries` back to 0, and at `triesBeforeStall` tries, which the server gives as the bound the views of recall functions stall at, `overtimesBeforeStall` of `@on.auto/recall`, twenty, the dataset stalls, phase `stalled`, its stall naming the event's record and the stop's words, the host notes `dataset_stalled` with the record and the words, and it folds nothing more until no active definition names its data part. A page reads, of the rows it changes, only those of the series and periods its events touch, one indexed row a pair, and learns which of its series are new with one probe a series over a `VALUES` list, `(SELECT 1 … LIMIT 1) IS NOT NULL`, which both stores run once a series and which reads at most one indexed row of it, so a page reads at most twice as many rows as it has events, 2,000 for 1,000, however many periods its series hold. The probe is not an `EXISTS`: PostgreSQL turns that into a join, and over a table it has no statistics of yet, as after a rebuild, into one that reads every row of the dataset, 126 ms for a page of 1,000 series over 1,000,000 rows against 11 ms for the probe and 47 to 52 ms for the `SELECT DISTINCT series … IN` it replaces; SQLite took 1.7 ms against 43 ms. A page's rows, its counts, its tries and its checkpoint are written in one transaction, which commits only if the checkpoint it read is still the row's.

`datasetsPortOn(database)` reads a dataset's row for `get_definition`'s `standing` (`src/capability/prediction-standing.ts`): `kind`, `data_sha256`, the phase, the checkpoint, the counts, the first and last period and `stall`, null until the dataset stalls and then `{ record, detail }`; a function that names an uploaded dataset stands as `{ kind: uploaded, sha256 }`.

## The files a build reads

`writtenDatasetFiles({ database, content, playbook, document, now })` writes the two files a build will read, through the content port, each kept under its SHA-256 (`src/files`): the scoring rows, the dense grid of the `window` periods that ends at the last period closed at `now`, and the training rows, the same without the last `holdout` periods. The header is `series,period`, the target and the features in the document's order; the rows are sorted by series, as their UTF-8 bytes sort, then by period, in CRLF records of RFC 4180; a period with no event is 0 for `count` and `sum` and empty for `last` and `mean`; a series observed in fewer than `holdout + 1` periods of the window is left out. It reads the dataset's row, the counts, the series and every row of both files in one snapshot of the host's database, `readConsistently`, so the two files are of the one checkpoint it names however far the fold moves on while they are written, and it never reads again. It refuses a dataset past 200,000 rows or 1,000 series, with the figures it counted, every series of the window observed often enough counted, or a file past 64 MiB, `too_large`, a dataset not yet live, not kept or stalled, `not_ready`, and a function that names an uploaded dataset, `uploaded`, each as a `DatasetFilesRefused` with its words, and a refusal keeps nothing: the scoring rows are written first, and the training rows are fewer, so the bytes can only be refused before anything is kept. No build calls it yet.

## The upload

`uploadDataset(content)` is the operation `upload_dataset`, `PUT /v1/orgs/{org}/playbooks/{playbook}/datasets/{sha256}` under `playbook:write`, served over HTTP alone, so it is no tool on any MCP endpoint. Its body is `text/csv` with a `Content-Length` of at most 64 MiB; it is read as a stream (`src/upload`), checked as UTF-8 and against the grammar of the files above with any target and feature names, CRLF or LF records and a leading byte order mark allowed, and kept through the content port with the path's digest expected. A body that breaks the grammar is refused `invalid_input` with the line of its first fault, one whose digest is not the path's with both digests, the body's the one the port computed as it kept the stream, and nothing is kept then. The answer is the digest, the bytes, the rows, the series, the columns, the grain and the first and last period.

## A run

`makePredictionFunctionAdapter({ pool, datasets, mostFunctions })` makes the capability, type `prediction`, media type `text/markdown`, guide `prediction-function`; the server gives it the computation pool, for the check, and the host's `DatasetsPort`. Its `mostActive` is `mostFunctions`, 32 unless the server's `PREDICTION_MAX_FUNCTIONS` says otherwise. A run (`src/run/prediction-run.ts`) refuses an input nested past 512 levels, one the question's or the build's schema refuses and a question whose series takes more than 256 bytes, `invalid_input` with the pointers; then a question is refused `conflict`, `no_active_version`, in the words "The function has no active version yet, and this runtime does not offer a build yet", and a build `unavailable`, `build_not_offered`. `finishesLaterFor` is false for a question, so a question's run cannot be cancelled from outside while its call is in flight, and true for anything else, and `longestRunFor` is 10 seconds for a question and, for a build or an input it cannot tell, the build's wait, `source.expires`, plus eight candidates scored at ten minutes each and a minute to start (`src/run/run-lengths.ts`). The capability declares no facts and no stream kinds of its own: `record_passed_over` is a note of the host, not a fact.

## Bounds

`predictionBounds` holds them: 1 to 8 horizons of 1 to 52 periods, a season of 2 to 366, a holdout of at least 8 and a window of at most 1,000, 8 filters, 40 features, names and the unit of 1 to 64 characters, 32 rules of 256 bytes, shares from 0.5 to 0.99, a shadow of 0 to 52 periods, a build's wait from one minute to 30 days, a series of 1 to 256 bytes, a dataset of 200,000 rows over 1,000 series in 64 MiB, a brief of 64 KiB rendered within the template engine's 200 ms, 10 seconds a question, two rebuilds at once a playbook and ten notes of passed-over events a dataset a page; the tries before a dataset stalls are the server's, the views' twenty. Each has a test that reaches it here, and the server's `src/prediction` reaches the upload's and the playbook's over HTTP.

## Measurements

`pnpm --filter @on.auto/prediction measure` measures, outside the tests (`measure.ts`, with its parts in `measure/`), on SQLite and, with `LEDGER_MEASURE_POSTGRESQL_URL` set, on PostgreSQL: 200,000 events of 1,000 series over 200 days, one an order a series a day, folded by the projector into the dataset of a daily function with two features, then 10,000 more appended while it is live, and the two files of a build's start written through the ledger's own content store and read back; then, on a database of its own, the same history folded into the dataset of the same function with a `data` test on its filter, `${ $data.total > 0 }`, which every event passes, tested on the filter thread of a pool, as the server tests it. Measured on 2026-10-12 on Node 26.10.0 on an Apple M4 Max, 16 cores, PostgreSQL 18.4 on the same machine, an x86_64 build run under translation, at a load average of 4 to 7.5:

A data test costs a page of 1,000 events about 63 ms on SQLite, about 0.06 ms an event on the filter thread, since the page prepares the dataset's tests once and tests each event in that session. When each event opened a session of its own and froze its context, the same rebuild with a data test folded 219 events a second on SQLite, 4,359 ms a page, measured over 20,000 events, so a page held the playbook's other folders, which the projector folds one after another, back by more than 4 s, and a rebuild of 200,000 events took about 15 minutes. The PostgreSQL rebuild without a data test varied from 13.1 s to 20.5 s over four runs at that load, and a rebuild that probed with an `EXISTS` from 9.9 s to 22.7 s over three. The live figure counts from the first append and includes the ten appends, which at the recording's rate take about 0.45 s on SQLite and 0.38 s on PostgreSQL. The server's `src/prediction/dataset-upload-over-http.test.ts` uploads a dataset of 67,000,182 bytes, 200,000 rows over 1,000 series and 41 columns, through HTTP on both stores and reads it back whole.

## Testing

`@on.auto/prediction/testing` exports `weeklyOrders`, the example document, `weeklyOrdersLines` and `weeklyOrdersWith(...replaced)`, which changes its lines, and `uploadedWeeklyOrders(sha256)`, the example over an uploaded dataset. The dataset's tests run on SQLite and, with `LEDGER_TEST_POSTGRESQL_URL` set, on PostgreSQL, each on a database of its own, through the suites in `src/dataset-testing`, which drive the host's projector with `projectedFolders` of `@on.auto/workflow-host/testing`; the run's tests use a fake datasets port and a fake `SavedDefinitions` that keeps content too (`src/testing/prediction-runs.ts`), and `src/testing/cancels-in-a-call.ts` cancels a run from outside while its call is in flight, through `cancel_run`, just before its call records its end.

## Source

`src/index.ts` is the entry point and `src/testing/index.ts` the entry point of the test support. `src/document` holds the document: its type, the front matter, the parts and parsing, and the derived schemas. `src/data` holds the data part, its filters, pointers, measures and digest, and `src/brief` the brief's variables, template and largest dataset. `src/periods` holds the calendar: civil days, grains and the moments an event's time is read as. `src/dataset` holds the folder, its tables and statements, the fold and the port; `src/files` the files a build reads; `src/upload` the upload's operation, its CSV reader and grammar, and the reading of a kept dataset's head. `src/capability` holds the capability, its bounds, its checks of the functions it calls and of an uploaded dataset, its standing, its words and the figures they print, and its guide, the public reference page, served to agents as `prediction-function`. `src/run` holds a run and its lengths. `src/dataset-testing` holds the dataset's suites and what they share.
